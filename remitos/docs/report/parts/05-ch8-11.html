8.4 Three findings

Nobody owns the routing decision. Read the table by row. Every player is a rail or a wallet. The "least-cost routing across rails" row is empty except for us, and it is empty for a structural reason: an incumbent rail has no incentive to build a product that compares it against competitors. Neutrality is not something bKash can credibly offer.

Compliance is the cost floor, and it is manual. The cost stack is dominated not by technology but by KYC labour, sanctions screening and pre-funding. Automating the first two and forecasting the third is where an AI-native entrant can actually move price — which is precisely the capability the Congress guide names for this challenge area.

The 49 % is not won on features. It is won on rate, speed, doorstep delivery and paperwork. Any feature that does not move one of those four converts nobody from the informal channel, however impressive it looks in a demo.

9How RemitOS overrides the existing process

"Override" does not mean replace. bKash will still be the wallet and the bank will still settle. What changes is who decides.

9.1 The positioning

The tempting pitch is "a better bKash". It is also the wrong one: distribution is saturated, the incumbent has fifteen years of agent build-out, and a new wallet competes on exactly the dimension where a new entrant is weakest.

The defensible position is the one nobody occupies: intelligence and orchestration above every rail, treating bKash, Nagad, Rocket, banks, agents and MTOs as interchangeable endpoints rather than as competitors.

9.2 Four specific overrides

Override 1 · The routing decision becomes computed rather than guessed

Today: the sender picks a provider, sees one price from one rail, and has no way to know whether another path would have delivered more. He compares three providers by hand, badly, every month.

With RemitOS: every feasible path is enumerated, its landed amount and arrival time predicted, the illegal ones removed, and the survivors ranked — with a one-line Bangla explanation of the trade-off. The user makes an informed choice in seconds, which is a decision he could not previously make at all.

Override 2 · Regulatory limits stop being a counter-side surprise

Today: quote, pay, pay out to wallet, discover the Tk 30,000 cash-out ceiling at the agent counter after a 40-minute journey.

With RemitOS: the ceiling is a constraint in the optimiser. The system proposes a legal split, prices both legs, and explains it in Bangla — before payment. See §6.5 and Figure 6.1.

This is the override that is most visible in thirty seconds on a stage, and the one that most clearly demonstrates a system that has read the regulation rather than only the API documentation.

Override 3 · Compliance becomes explainable and fast

Today: a held transfer produces silence, then a customer-service queue, then — sometimes — an explanation. Silence during a held emergency transfer is the single most effective driver of a household back to hundi.

With RemitOS: an immediate notification, a plain-Bangla reason, a stated SLA, and a visible appeal route to a human. Behind it, the decision is grounded in cited regulation — and if no governing rule can be retrieved with sufficient confidence, the system refuses to decide and escalates rather than guessing.

Override 4 · The payout container becomes a choice rather than an accident

Today: money lands wherever the chosen channel happens to deliver, and moving it costs another fee and another trip.

With RemitOS: the destination is part of the optimisation. Binimoy already provides the rails to move between banks, MFS and PSPs;[17][19] what has been missing is anything choosing on the recipient's behalf.

The override in one sentence We do not move money better than bKash. We decide better than anyone currently decides — and in a market where the decision is presently made by guesswork roughly half the time, that is where the value is.

10.2 What we cannot do at all

Outbound payments from Bangladesh. This is worth restating as the clearest limit. Bangladesh restricts outbound transactions, which is why PayPal is unavailable and why Xoom is inbound and non-commercial only.[25][27][28] No application architecture changes a capital-account policy. Our freelancer persona's core problem — receiving commercial payments and paying for foreign services — is only partially addressable, and we say so.

Beating the kerb rate. Hundi's rate advantage derives from operating outside the official exchange mechanism. We can minimise every controllable cost. We cannot match a rate that is available precisely because it is not legal.

Out-building agent density. Fifteen years and hundreds of thousands of touchpoints. Partnership is the only route.

10.3 The cold-start problem, stated properly

Every model in this system needs data we do not yet have, and the honest consequences differ by model:

ModelCold-start realityLaunch behaviour
Corridor cost predictorNo own settlement historyPublished fee schedules only, labelled provisional in the UI
AML risk scoringNo adjudicated alerts, so no labelsModel does not exist. Rules-only, widened human review
Fraud detectionNo confirmed cases; fraudsters find new products fastDeterministic rules plus graph anomaly detection
FX forecastingPublic history exists — the least affectedOperational, with wide intervals stated
Bangla NLURegional speech corpora are scarce and must be builtLimited intents; expanded as evaluated coverage grows
The tempting shortcut, and why we refuse it The obvious fix is to train on synthetic data and report the resulting accuracy. It would produce impressive numbers for a submission.

It would also be a fabricated result presented as real, which Congress §20.4 prohibits outright. Synthetic data in this project exists only to exercise pipelines, is labelled synthetic on every surface that renders it, and never produces a quoted metric. There is no accuracy figure anywhere in this report, because none has been measured.

10.4 What might be wrong with our thesis

Three ways the central argument could fail, stated as they would be by a sceptical judge:

1. The incumbent simply builds it. bKash could add routing intelligence. The counter-argument is structural rather than optimistic: a rail has no incentive to build a product that ranks it against competitors, and neutrality cannot be retrofitted onto a party with a stake in the answer. That said, bKash could partner with an independent layer — somebody else's independent layer — and this remains the largest single commercial risk in the project. It carries a named owner in the risk register.

2. Partners refuse to cooperate. MFS operators have limited incentive to help a layer that commoditises them. The counter-arguments — incremental inbound volume they do not currently capture, and formalising flow that presently goes to hundi — are real but not guaranteed to persuade. If no payout partner agrees, the product does not exist.

3. The routing gains are smaller than assumed. If, in practice, corridor prices are tightly clustered, the value of choosing between them shrinks. We do not yet know, because we have not measured it — and the corridor cost scraper (n8n workflow 04) exists specifically to find out during Phase 3 validation rather than to assume.

11.2 Bangla as the source language

Covered in §6.4 as a remedy; restated here as a design discipline. Bangla strings drive the layout, English fits it. Bengali-complete typography chosen before the Latin face. Lakh/crore grouping by default. Bengali numerals selectable and on by default. A financial glossary written by native speakers rather than translated. Every AI-generated explanation produced in Bangla rather than generated in English and translated — because translating an explanation reliably loses the explanation.

11.3 Voice as a primary interface

For a recipient with limited literacy, voice is not an accessibility enhancement. It is the interface.

11.4 The IVR and USSD path

A recipient with a feature phone gets a Bangla voice call announcing arrival, a menu for balance and nearest agent, and — critically — spoken limit guidance before she travels: "আজ এজেন্ট থেকে সর্বোচ্চ ৩০ হাজার টাকা তুলতে পারবেন".

That single sentence, delivered before a 40-minute journey rather than at the counter after it, is the entire argument of ADR 0007 rendered for someone who cannot read it.

11.6 Accessibility and trust

  • Icon-led navigation. Every action carries an icon, never text alone.
  • Elder mode. Enlarged targets and type, higher contrast, fewer items per screen.
  • WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and target sizing, with semantic labels in both languages.
  • Agent-assisted mode with the agent's identity logged on every action — protecting honest agents as much as deterring dishonest ones.
  • Scam warnings that are concrete and in Bangla ("Have you spoken to this person by voice today?"), dismissible by an adult sending their own money, and not repeated so often that people learn to tap through them.
  • No amounts or names in lock-screen previews — many phones in this market are shared.
The design test we apply Every screen and every flow is checked against one question: could Rahima Begum — 58, limited literacy, feature phone, 40 minutes from an agent — complete this without asking anyone for help?

Where the answer is no, the flow is not finished. Where it cannot be yes in the app, the IVR path must carry it.
