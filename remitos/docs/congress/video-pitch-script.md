# 3-Minute Preliminary Video Pitch — Script

**Deadline:** 31 October 2026, 12:00 PM BST (§3 timeline)
**Format:** exactly 180 seconds, five segments per §9.2

> Timings are tight by design. Rehearse to the second — going over on segment 2 means
> cutting the demo, which is the segment judges weight most.

---

## 0:00 – 0:30 · Problem
*Evaluators look for: clarity, relevance*

> A construction worker in Riyadh sends forty-five thousand taka home to his mother every
> month. He doesn't know which of three providers will actually deliver the most money.
> Neither does anyone else — because nobody computes it.
>
> Bangladesh received **thirty-five and a half billion dollars** in remittances last
> fiscal year. Seventh in the world. And the World Bank puts the global cost of sending
> money at **six point three six percent** against a UN target of three.
>
> Here is the number that matters: roughly **half** of the money coming into Bangladesh
> doesn't come through the formal system at all. It comes through hundi. Because hundi is
> faster, and the rate is better, and nobody asks for paperwork.

**On screen:** the four numbers, one at a time. No stock footage of money.

---

## 0:30 – 1:00 · Solution
*Evaluators look for: simplicity, uniqueness*

> We are not building another wallet. bKash, Nagad and the banks are rails — and rails
> are not the gap.
>
> The gap is the **decision**. Which rail, which exchange window, which payout point
> delivers the most taka, soonest, legally. Today that decision is made by a guess.
>
> RemitOS is an intelligence layer that makes it. Three capabilities: machine-learned
> routing across every rail, compliance automation grounded in the actual regulation with
> citations, and predictive send-timing — delivered in Bangla, and reachable on a feature
> phone with no internet.

**On screen:** the rails diagram — RemitOS above, every rail below and interchangeable.

---

## 1:00 – 2:00 · Demo / Flow
*Evaluators look for: feasibility, logic*

> He enters forty-five thousand taka. We show three routes — and we lead with what
> actually reaches her, not with the fee. Two hours longer, three hundred and twenty taka
> more. In Bangla, in one line.
>
> Now watch the case nobody designs for. He sends **one hundred and twenty thousand** —
> a medical emergency. Bangladesh Bank caps agent cash-out at thirty thousand a day.
>
> Every other product quotes it, takes his money, and his mother finds out at the counter
> — after a forty-minute journey, during an emergency.
>
> We know the limit **before** we quote. So we propose a legal split: thirty thousand to
> the wallet today, ninety to her bank account tomorrow. Both priced. Both explained.
> And she hears it as a **voice call in Bangla**, because she does not have a smartphone.

**On screen:** the actual J3 split screen. This is the sixty seconds that wins or loses.

---

## 2:00 – 2:30 · AI Approach
*Evaluators look for: depth, structure*

> Four AI layers, and none of them are a chatbot.
>
> **Machine learning** predicts landed cost and arrival time per rail. **Retrieval** over
> Bangladesh Bank circulars and FATF guidance answers compliance questions — and if it
> cannot find the rule, it refuses and calls a human. It never guesses at regulation.
>
> A **knowledge graph** catches what per-transaction scoring cannot: the network
> signature of hundi agent activity. And **agents** investigate alerts and build the
> evidence file — but no agent moves money. Every irreversible action has a human gate.
>
> Personal data never leaves Bangladesh. We tokenise before any external model call, and
> an egress filter blocks it if we get it wrong.

**On screen:** the four layers, then the residency boundary diagram.

---

## 2:30 – 3:00 · Impact & Next Step
*Evaluators look for: vision, potential*

> Every one percent of cost removed from a thirty-five billion dollar flow is
> **three hundred and fifty million dollars a year** staying with Bangladeshi families.
>
> We measure landed amount per hundred dollars sent, time to the recipient's hand, and
> the false-positive rate **broken down by group** — because a held transfer is somebody's
> medicine, and it should not be held more often because she lives in a village.
>
> The core is domain-agnostic. Credit, fraud, wealth and SME lending are configuration,
> not rewrites.
>
> Next: Bangladesh Bank sandbox, one corridor, real money, real users.
>
> **From Bangladesh to the world.**

## Production notes

| | |
|---|---|
| **Do** | Show the real screen. Speak plainly. State the limits honestly. Lead with landed amount everywhere |
| **Do not** | Show a chatbot. Say "revolutionary". Fabricate a metric. Fill time with an architecture diagram nobody can read in four seconds |
| Language | English narration with Bangla subtitles, and the product visibly in Bangla |
| The 0:30 arithmetic | 1 % of USD 35.56 bn ≈ USD 355 m. Stated as an arithmetic illustration of the flow's scale, **not** as a claimed saving |

That last line matters. §20.4 prohibits misleading claims, and the difference between
"one percent of this flow is USD 355 m" and "we will save USD 355 m" is the difference
between an honest pitch and a disqualifiable one.
